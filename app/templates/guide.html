Guide and legend

What the terms on these pages mean, how the pieces fit together, and what good results would (and would not) look like.

What we are going forThe practice loop RV termsEEG termsReading the graph BadgesReading the numbers

What we are going for

Two separate things, deliberately kept apart:

  1. A repeatable practice. Do blind remote-viewing sessions in a consistent way, record what you sketched and how sure you were, and keep every session so you can look back. The headband adds a record of your physiological state: were you calm and inwardly focused, or tense and distracted?
  2. Honest measurement. Find out, over many trials, whether your impressions match targets better than chance, and whether your confidence tells you anything. The app is built so the result cannot be flattered: the target stays hidden until you submit, and the judging mode compares you to a fixed chance level ({{ chance_pct }}% with {{ n_word }} options).

What a good outcome looks like

  • A consistent starting state: a similar calm, low-beta state at the start of each session, and fewer artifacts in the quality notes. Whether that state helps your results is something the trial statistics can show over time; the EEG alone cannot.
  • Calibration: your confidence rises when you are more accurate (a positive confidence/accuracy correlation) and falls when you are not.
  • Beating chance, if it is real: a judging hit rate clearly above chance ({{ chance_pct }}% with {{ n_word }} options) over dozens of trials, with a small p-value. A handful of trials cannot show this either way.

What it can not do

  • EEG band power cannot tell you whether a viewing was correct. A high "RV index" is a brain state, not a hit.
  • The "intuitive windows" are simply the highest points of a smoothed line. Some highest points always exist, even in random noise.
  • Self-scored accuracy is subjective. Prefer the judging hit rate when you want an objective number.

The practice loop

  1. Record with your headband (Muse Monitor, Mind Monitor, or the experimental recorder in tools/). Press the app's marker button at moments that matter, such as "start viewing" and "sketching".
  2. Run a trial under RV targets: get a coordinate, sketch and write impressions, rate confidence, submit. Do this while the headband is recording.
  3. Reveal and score. See the target next to your sketches, then score the match 0 to 100 and note what you got right, what was noise, and what you would do differently.
  4. Upload the recording on the EEG sessions page. You get the graph, the quality notes and written feedback from your AI coach.
  5. Review over time. Trends across sessions, your trial statistics, and your own notes are where patterns appear. Single sessions are noisy.

RV terms (the Targets pages)

Coordinate
A random tag such as 4829-1736. It carries no information about the target; it is just a label you focus on, as in classic controlled remote-viewing protocols.
Target
The photo you are trying to describe. It is chosen when the trial starts but is never sent to your browser before you submit.
Blind
You cannot see the target, its title, or its source until you submit. The server enforces it, and a stored fingerprint (SHA-256) shows the target was fixed before you began.
Impressions
Your written notes: shapes, colours, textures, temperatures, motion, feelings. Sketch first, then label.
Sketch pages
Up to six whiteboard pages per trial, each saved as a picture.
Confidence
0 to 100, entered before the reveal: how sure you are that your session captured the target.
Self-scored accuracy
0 to 100, entered after the reveal: how well your session matched. 0 means nothing matched, 100 means essentially the target. Count only what you wrote or drew before the reveal.
Tournament judging
Optional. After you submit, you pick which of {{ n_word }} images matches your session best (the target plus {{ 'one decoy' if n_opts == 2 else (n_opts - 1)|string + ' decoys' }} from {{ 'a different category' if n_opts == 2 else 'different categories' }}) before the reveal.
Decoy
A wrong option in judging mode, from a different category than the target.
Hit rate
The share of judged trials where you picked the real target. Chance is {{ chance_pct }}% with {{ n_word }} options.
p-value
The probability of getting at least this many hits by pure luck (an exact test against chance, {{ chance_pct }}% per trial with {{ n_word }} options; trials judged with a different number of options are each compared with their own chance level). Small (conventionally under 0.05) means unlikely to be luck. With few trials it will be large even if you are doing well.
Confidence vs accuracy (r)
Correlation, from -1 to +1, between your confidence and your self-scored accuracy. Near 0 means your confidence does not predict your results; positive means it does.
Category
The theme of the target (water, tower, desert, ice and so on). The hub breaks results down by category to show where you do better.
Source and licence
Shown at the reveal: who took the photo, where it came from (Wikimedia Commons or Pexels), and its licence.

Brainwave capture during a trial

On the trial page you can connect your Muse over Bluetooth, record while you view and sketch, and see the graph right after the reveal.

Capture
The app listens to the headband in your browser (Web Bluetooth, Chrome on Android or desktop). It needs a secure page: HTTPS, or localhost. It is optional; the trial works without it.
Contact dots
One per sensor: good is a clean signal, fair is usable, noisy means a loose fit or muscle tension, no signal means the sensor is not touching skin. Fix these before you start.
Live bars
A rough picture of the five bands right now. They are for checking the headband works, not for judging your session.
Markers
Tags on the timeline: capture started, first stroke and submitted are added for you; Begin viewing, Impression and Sketching are buttons you press. They split the graph into phases you can compare.
Blind protocol
The recording is stored with the trial but its graph and AI feedback stay locked until the reveal, so nothing about the target can leak into the analysis.
Scale
Values are log10 of power in µV², close to but not guaranteed identical to Muse Monitor. Compare sessions recorded the same way, not across the two sources.

The graph's own key (every line and word on it) sits right under the graph on the Sessions and trial pages.

EEG terms (the Sessions pages)

Muse sensors
AF7 and AF8 sit on the forehead; TP9 and TP10 sit behind the ears. The app averages all four. Forehead sensors also pick up eye blinks and frowning.
Delta
Roughly 1 to 4 Hz. Deep sleep; in a waking recording, often eye movement and blinks.
Theta
Roughly 4 to 8 Hz. Drowsy, dreamy, inward-focused states. The main band for this app.
Alpha
Roughly 8 to 13 Hz. Relaxed wakefulness, typically strongest with eyes closed.
Beta
Roughly 13 to 30 Hz. Active thinking and alertness; also tension and muscle activity.
Gamma
Roughly 30 to 44 Hz. Fast activity; with a dry headband it is often muscle noise.
Band power
How strong each band is over time. Muse Monitor exports it on a logarithmic scale, so differences between bands are ratios and values can be negative. Only compare against your own baseline.
Smoothing window
A moving average over this many consecutive data points, to calm the jitter. Larger is smoother but blurs short events. Default 7. Counted in data points, not seconds; Muse exports have several points per second.
RV index
Smoothed theta minus beta. Higher means more theta relative to beta: a relaxed, inward-focused state. Compare this number between sessions.
RV index (z)
The RV index re-scaled within one session (mean 0, spread 1), so the graph can show it next to the bands. Its average is zero by construction, so never compare it between sessions.
Intuitive window
One of the highest peaks of the z-scored RV index. Top windows sets how many to mark; Min gap keeps them at least that many seconds apart. They are moments of interest to review, not detections.
Marker
A button press during recording (shown as a dotted vertical line). Use it to label the phases of a session.
Feedback score
When an Excel file includes a Feedback sheet, these are your scores for moments or intervals. With at least 10 scored points the app looks for the RV-index level that best separates your good moments from the rest.
Threshold (Youden J)
That best-separating level. J runs from 0 (no better than guessing) to 1 (perfect separation). Treat it as exploratory with small samples.
Quality notes
Warnings the app raises before interpretation: a short recording, the headband off, poor sensor contact, lots of blinks or jaw clenches, data gaps.
Blinks / jaw clenches
Counts from the headband's own detection. Many of them mean frontal theta and high-frequency beta or gamma may be artifacts, not brain activity.
Horseshoe (HSI)
Sensor contact quality, per sensor: 1 is good, 4 is bad.
AI coach
A language model on your own machine that reads a compact numeric summary of the session, your earlier sessions and your notes, and writes feedback. It never sees the raw EEG and is instructed not to claim the data proves anything. The exact text it was given is saved with each session (ai_prompt.txt).

Reading the session graph

Alpha, Theta, Beta
The smoothed band powers (solid lines, labelled in the legend under the graph).
RV index (z)
The dashed line: theta minus beta, re-scaled. Peaks mean more theta relative to beta.
Dots labelled "Window N"
The intuitive windows, numbered from highest, with their clock time.
Dotted vertical lines
Your markers, with their labels.
RV index vs feedback plot
Appears when feedback scores exist: each dot is a scored moment, and the dashed line is the best-separating threshold.
Trend graph
The mean raw theta-minus-beta per session, so you can see whether your typical state is shifting over weeks.

Badges

pending
Analysis or AI feedback is still running.
done
Finished.
failed
Something went wrong; the page shows what, and re-analyze or regenerate retries it.
assigned
A trial is open and the target is hidden. Sketch and submit.
judging
Submitted; pick one of the images to lock in your choice.
revealed
The target is visible; score it.
complete
Scored and saved.
abandoned
You gave up on the trial; the target may be revealed.

Reading the numbers

NumberIt can tell youIt can not tell you
RV index (raw)How relaxed and inward-focused you were, compared with your other sessionsWhether your impressions were right
Intuitive windowsWhere to look back in the recordingThat something happened there
Self-scored accuracyHow you felt the match wentAnything objective; it is open to hindsight bias
Judging hit rate and p-valueWhether you beat chance across the trials you didAnything useful from fewer than about 20 judged trials
Confidence vs accuracy (r)Whether your gut sense of certainty is informativeCause; a few trials give a wildly unstable value

Rules of thumb: aim for dozens of trials before drawing conclusions, decide in advance how many you will run, and count every trial, including the ones you would rather forget.
