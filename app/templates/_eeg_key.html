Key: what the lines and words mean

The five brainwave bands

Your brain's electrical activity, sorted by how fast it wobbles (Hz = wobbles per second). Click a band's name above the graph to show or hide it.

Delta 1–4 Hz

Slowest waves. Strong in deep sleep.

Awake, a rise is usually eye blinks or movement, not deep thought.

Theta 4–8 Hz

Slow, dreamy waves. Drowsy, daydreaming, turned inward.

Rises when you drift inward or get sleepy. The main band for remote viewing practice.

Alpha 8–13 Hz

Relaxed, idle waves. Calm but awake.

Rises when you close your eyes and relax; drops when you concentrate or open your eyes.

Beta 13–30 Hz

Busy, alert waves. Thinking, analysing, being on guard.

Rises with concentration, worry, or tense face and jaw muscles.

Gamma 30–44 Hz

Fastest waves. Hard to read on a headband like this.

Spikes are usually muscle tension (jaw, forehead), not brain activity.

The terms on the graph

Calm-focus meter
Theta minus beta (the "RV index"). Up means more slow, inward activity than busy, alert activity; down means the reverse. It describes your state, not whether a viewing was right.
Session average (“avg”, “usual”)
The graph compares everything with your own average in this one recording. The number shown (for example +1.2) is how many “typical swings” above (+) or below (−) your average you were (a z-score). Around ±1 is a clear change; near 0 is ordinary.
Phase (shaded strip)
The stretch of time between two markers, such as “begin viewing” to “sketching”. Compare phases in the data table.
Marker (dotted line)
A moment you tagged, or one the app added automatically (capture started, first stroke, submitted).
Window (numbered dot)
One of the highest points of the meter, numbered from the highest. A spot to look back at, not a detection. Some highest points always exist, even in random noise.
Smoothing
The lines are averaged over a few seconds so jitter doesn't hide the shape. Short blips are blurred on purpose.
Contact and quality notes
Orange notes under the graph warn about a loose headband, blinks, jaw clenching or data gaps. When they appear, trust the lines less.
Gap
A break in a line means data was missing (for example the headband disconnected).
